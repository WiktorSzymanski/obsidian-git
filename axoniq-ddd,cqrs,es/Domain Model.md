# `Domain Model`

The term `Domain Model` refers to a `Model` of given `Domain`.

You are creating software to solve specific challenges in a given `Domain`. Your solution must operate in that `Domain` and comply with everything in that `Domain`. To achieve this, you must discuss various aspects of the `Domain` that are **relevant** to your solution with various people. Therefore, creating a `Domain Model` that represents those aspects is crucial for the people involved to know, understand and simulate the actual `Domain`.

You should consider any part of the actual `Domain` to be **relevant** if:

- It directly or indirectly helps you solve the problem(s) at hand.
- It must be taken into account even though it does not help solve the problem _(an example is regulations)_.

Any other aspect of the actual `Domain` should be left out of the `Domain Model`. Your goal is to have a model that is useful, not one that is 100% correct. To rephrase George Box’s quote from the previous chapter:

> Remember that your domain model will be wrong; the practical question is how wrong it has to be not to be useful.
